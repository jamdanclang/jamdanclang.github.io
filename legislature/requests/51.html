If I were a legislative liaison, what would I need to know about this session?

2026-08-30 19:56:34 · Sessions 109B · standard

Nebraska Legislature 109th Legislature, 2nd Session

As a legislative liaison tracking the 109th Legislature, Second Session (109B) with a focus on the Department of Economic Development (DED), you need to be aware of significant mid-biennium budget adjustments, major programmatic and policy transformations, and several structural reporting modifications.

1. Mid-Biennium Budget & Appropriation Adjustments (LB1071 / LB1209 / LB1167)

Major funding allocations and statutory earmarks across DED's core programs have been modified:

  • Community and Rural Development (Program 601):
  • Funding Levels: Appropriations are adjusted to \$7,294,942 General Funds, \$21,671,030 Cash Funds, and \$54,632,192 Federal Funds for FY2026-27 [1-5]. Unexpended Cash Fund balances for several acts, including the Rural Workforce Housing Investment Act and the Nebraska Affordable Housing Act, are reappropriated [6-9].
  • Statutory Earmarks: Earmarks include \$250,000 Cash Funds in FY2025-26 from the Middle Income Workforce Housing Investment Fund to study prefabricated housing [1-3, 10], \$700,000 General Funds in both FY25-26 and FY26-27 for economic development districts [1-3, 10], and \$4,880,762 General Funds to aid mentorship programs [1-3, 10]. Under LB1190, the criteria for these mentorship programs are expanded to cover nonprofit organizations that either improve reading levels or promote professional growth for elementary students [1-3, 10, 11]. Earmarks also include \$1,000,000 General Funds in FY25-26 (and \$-0- in FY26-27) for the outdoor camp grant authorized in section 81-1213.04 [6-9].
  • Industrial Recruitment (Program 603):
  • Funding Levels: Adjusted to \$6,195,537 General Funds, \$72,525,784 Cash Funds, and \$21,188,298 Federal Funds for FY2026-27 [12-14].
  • State Aid & Earmarks: Earmarks include \$50,000,000 Cash Funds from the Nebraska Transformational Project Fund for state aid in both FY25-26 and FY26-27 [14-16], and \$4,000,000 Cash Funds in FY25-26 from the ImagiNE Nebraska Revolving Loan Fund [14-16].
  • Microenterprise & Business Innovation: Under LB1167, the department is directed to utilize \$11,020,352 General Funds in FY26-27 for Business Innovation Act grants, with \$3,000,000 specifically earmarked for the microenterprise assistance program [17, 18].
  • Economic Recovery (Program 611):
  • Funding Levels: Allocated \$31,318,519 Cash Funds in FY25-26 and \$31,378,477 Cash Funds in FY26-27 [19-22].
  • State Aid: Earmarks \$27,448,770 Cash Funds each year for state aid [20-22]. Unexpended balances from the federal Coronavirus Capital Projects Fund and the Economic Development Administration's Travel, Tourism and Outdoor Recreation program are reappropriated [20-22].

2. Major Programmatic Overhauls & Policy Shifts

  • Key Employer and Jobs Retention Act & Site and Building Development Act (LB1165):
  • Corporate Mergers: Expands the definition of "change of ownership and control" and "key employer" to include Nebraska-headquartered entities that merge or combine with out-of-state entities, in an effort to protect large numbers of Nebraska-based jobs [23-26].
  • Capital Improvement Grants: Intends to transfer \$5,000,000 from the General Fund to the Site and Building Development Fund in FY26-27 solely to provide capital improvement grants (at a rate of \$5 per square foot, capped at \$5 million) to key employers undergoing a change in ownership [26-35].
  • First-Class Cities Relief: Authorizes DED to use the Site and Building Development Fund to award grants or zero-interest loans (up to \$2.5 million per fiscal year in FY26-27 and FY27-28) to cities of the first class with under 50,000 inhabitants impacted by sudden, significant private-sector entity closures or downsizing [35-41].
  • Business Innovation Act (BIA) & Microenterprise Reform (LB1044 / LB1205 / LB1015 / LB47):
  • Mandatory Allocations & Funding Floor: LB1044 streamlines administrative processes and transitions the BIA from discretionary spending to a core fiscal priority by establishing a \$8,000,000 annual funding floor for specific grant programs and signaling a long-term commitment of \$15,000,000 in annual appropriations starting in FY26-27 [109B-42]. It also removes gubernatorial approval from the department's biennial action plans [109B-42].
  • Microenterprise Assistance: Amends section 81-12,162 under LB1205 to remove DED's administrative flexibility, now requiring the department to issue microenterprise assistance grants annually (up to \$3 million per year) [43-45]. Under LB47 and AM2450, these microenterprise assistance grants are focused on distressed areas with administrative expenses capped at 5% [46-50].
  • Business Innovation Cash Fund: LB1015 establishes a new Cash Fund within the BIA, funded by a designated percentage of state unemployment insurance tax collections, which requires DED to supply matching financial information to the Department of Labor [51-53].
  • New Taxpayer Recruitment Grant Act (LB1152):
  • Targeted Recruitment: Directs DED to establish and administer a new grant program allowing political subdivisions and economic/community development nonprofits to apply for funds to recruit out-of-state households to relocate to Nebraska [54-57]. The department is authorized to adopt rules and regulations to carry out this recruitment program [58, 59].

3. Housing and Community Development Reforms

  • Workforce Housing Investment Acts (LB1072 / LB768 / LB819 / LB1067):
  • Fund Sweep and Consolidation: LB1072 sweeps the remaining unobligated balances of the Middle Income Workforce Housing Investment Fund and the Rural Workforce Housing Investment Fund, transferring them to the Affordable Housing Trust Fund before June 30, 2026 [60, 61]. It authorizes future transfers between these funds at the Legislature's direction [109B-61].
  • Extended Sunsets: LB819 extends the sunset dates for both the Rural and Middle Income Workforce Housing Investment Acts to 2037 and raises their respective cost limitations [109B-62].
  • Agent Contracting: LB768 explicitly authorizes DED to contract with a statewide nonprofit organization or state instrumentality to act as the department's agent in administering the Rural Workforce Housing Investment Act [63-65] and the Affordable Housing Act [64, 65].
  • Documentary Stamp Tax: LB1067 proposes to increase the Documentary Stamp Tax and distribute the increased revenues equally to the Rural and Middle Income Workforce Housing Investment Funds [66, 67].
  • Community Development Assistance Act (CDAA) Certifications (LB650):
  • Certification Suspension: While DED continues to administer the CDAA tax credits, section 53 specifically prohibits the DED director from approving any program certification applications for eligible businesses for calendar years 2026 through 2029 [68-72]. Continuous certifications (up to \$6 million annually) are scheduled to resume in calendar year 2030 [68-72].

4. Structural, Administrative, and Reporting Changes

  • Reporting Streamlining (LB1048 / LB1165):
  • Agreement Disclosures: LB1048 changes the frequency of DED’s annual report detailing agreements and taxpayer identities to a biennial schedule, presented on or before December 15 of even-numbered years [73-75].
  • Joint Reporting: Under the Urban Redevelopment Act, DED and the Department of Revenue must now electronically submit a joint annual report to the Legislature by July 15 instead of October 31 [76-81]. A similar joint annual report is required under the Grow the Good Life Act on a joint fiscal year, accrual basis by October 31 of each year, with a joint appearance before the Appropriations and Revenue Committees on or before December 15 [82-84].
  • Innovation Hub (iHub) Fee Restructuring (LB1072):
  • Redirects iHub application fees collected under section 81-12,110 away from the Innovation Hub Cash Fund, redirecting them to the DED Administrative Cash Fund [61, 85-88].
  • Tourism Commission Consolidation (LB1142 / LR310):
  • Reintegration Proposed: LB1142 proposes to return the entire administration of the Nebraska Visitors Development Act from the independent Tourism Commission back to DED [89, 90]. Additionally, LR310 authorizes an interim study to evaluate the feasibility of consolidating the independent Tourism Commission back into DED to optimize brand identity, administrative efficiency, and access to DED financing tools [91, 92].
  • Super Sites and Mega Sites Study (Site and Building Development Fund):
  • Directs the DED director to appoint an advisory committee representing economic development organizations across each of Nebraska’s three congressional districts to assist DED in identifying and evaluating potential super sites (500–1,000 acres) and mega sites (over 1,000 acres) to attract major investment [27, 29, 31, 33, 41, 93].
