Nebraska Legislature 107th Legislature, 2nd Session

Senator Brett Lindstrom advanced several major fiscal initiatives during the 107th Legislature, 2nd Session. He introduced LB825, which successfully proposed phasing out state taxation of federal Social Security benefits by 2025; he moved for cloture on the bill in March 2022 [1-3]. His Travel Insurance Act (LB728) was enacted through its inclusion in LB863 [4, 5], and his proposal to redefine securities under the Public Funds Deposit Security Act (LB826) was integrated into LB707 [6, 7].

Lindstrom also introduced the Growing Our Workforce Investment Now Act (LB730), which served as a primary vehicle for extending historic preservation and school readiness tax credits [8-10]. He advanced LB729, creating the Quick Action Closing Fund Act to incentivize high-impact business projects [11, 12]. Furthermore, he introduced LB919 to expand the eligible program area for sales tax revenue turnbacks under the Sports Arena Facility Financing Assistance Act [13-15]. While LB891, addressing school levy limitations, was introduced, it did not advance as significantly [16].

Nebraska Legislature 107th Legislature, 1st Session

Senator Brett Lindstrom successfully introduced several major legislative measures. LB64 (approved May 25, 2021) initiated a phased-in state income tax exemption for Social Security benefits, reaching a full deduction over a ten-year period [1-3]. He also authored LB39 (approved May 25, 2021), expanding the Sports Arena Facility Financing Assistance Act to authorize assistance for sports complexes and multipurpose competitive fields [4, 5].

Administrative reform was achieved through LB63 (approved May 5, 2021), which moved the deadline for permissive property tax exemption recommendations to March 1 [6-8]. LB38, which proposed technical corrections regarding retaliatory taxes and tax credits for insurers, advanced from the Revenue Committee but was not enacted in the session's recorded slip laws [9-11]. Lindstrom also co-introduced LR14, applying for a convention of states to propose constitutional amendments [12, 13], and LR109, recognizing the "Battle of the Joshes" for its charitable contributions to the Food Bank of Lincoln and Children's Hospital [14, 15].

Nebraska Legislature 106th Legislature, 2nd Session

Senator Brett Lindstrom secured significant legislative wins during the session, notably the enactment of LB 266, which expanded the School Readiness Tax Credit Act to include self-employed child care providers [1, 2]. He also co-introduced LB 1124, the Opioid Prevention and Treatment Act, which was approved by the Governor on August 7, 2020 [3, 4].

Lindstrom effectively advanced several policy priorities by integrating them into larger legislative vehicles. LB 954 (dental fee restrictions) and LB 1199 (motor vehicle service insurance) were incorporated into LB 774 [5, 6]. Additionally, LB 929 (real estate exemptions) and LB 1123 (student loans as security) were merged into LB 808 [7], while LB 764 (fiduciary investments) was folded into LB 909 [8].

He also moved the Infrastructure Improvement and Replacement Assistance Act (LB 242) to General File, proposing a sales tax "turnback" for municipal water projects [9, 10]. Furthermore, Lindstrom co-sponsored the ImagiNE Nebraska Act (LB 1107), which modernized the state's economic development platform [11, 12]. He withdrew LB 765 by motion in February 2020 [13].

Nebraska Legislature 106th Legislature, 1st Session

During the 106th Legislature, Senator Brett Lindstrom successfully passed several significant measures focused on finance, technology, and taxation. Key accomplishments include the Online Notary Public Act (LB186, approved 5/30/2019), which authorized remote notarial acts via communication technology [1, 2], and the College Savings Tax Credit Act (LB610, approved 5/30/2019), incentivizing employer contributions to 529 plans [3, 4].

He secured passage of LB218 (approved 5/29/2019), redefining tangible personal property to exclude certain political subdivision utility facilities [5, 6], and LB259 (approved 3/07/2019), updating the Securities Act of Nebraska [7, 8]. Other successes include LB603 (approved 4/24/2019) regarding ATM fees [9, 10] and LB177 (approved 5/08/2019), extending bonding authority for natural resources districts [11, 12].

Lindstrom also navigated LB310 (approved 2/19/2020) to set deadlines for historic preservation tax credits [13, 14] and LB266 (approved 8/17/2020) to update the School Readiness Tax Credit Act [15, 16]. Conversely, his proposal to adjust installment loan interest rates (LB188) did not pass, facing an indefinite postponement motion in May 2019 [17, 18].

Nebraska Legislature 105th Legislature, 2nd Session

During the 105th Legislature, 2nd Session, Senator Brett Lindstrom successfully championed several key measures. LB 738, approved April 17, 2018, indexed state Social Security tax brackets for inflation [1, 2]. He secured passage of LB 743, adopting the Public Adjusters Licensing Act to regulate property claim settlements and enhance consumer protections [3, 4]. LB 742 was enacted to clarify court authority to reform unreasonable noncompete agreements for Nebraska-based franchisors [5-7].

Lindstrom’s LB 855, which provided for the sealing of criminal records after a pardon, was incorporated into the successful LB 1132 [8-10]. Additionally, his LB 740 proposal allowing the Metropolitan Utilities District to register fleet vehicles permanently was enacted via LB 909 [11, 12].

Proposals that advanced to General File but did not pass include LB 811, modernizing credit card fraud statutes for computer chip technology, and LB 812/813, updating securities and financial institution regulations [13-15]. He also initiated interim studies on appraiser act updates (LR 352) and workforce financial mobility (LR 413) [16, 17].

Nebraska Legislature 105th Legislature, 1st Session

As Chairperson of the Banking, Commerce and Insurance Committee, Senator Brett Lindstrom successfully steered a significant body of financial and regulatory legislation during the 105th Legislature [1-3]. Lindstrom introduced and achieved passage for LB 138, which enabled uniform transfer-on-death registration for business interests [4-6], and LB 306, which modernized the Portable Electronics Insurance Act (Approved May 10, 2017) [7-9]. Other successful regulatory measures he introduced included LB 184 regarding loan broker contracts [10, 11], LB 185 establishing abandoned license application procedures [12, 13], and LB 186 updating money transmitter surety bonds [14, 15].

He also co-introduced LB 641, creating the Bioscience Innovation Program [16, 17]. While his standalone tax restructure (LB 452) and Social Security tax adjustment (LB 453) did not pass, his economic development and affordable housing initiatives (LB 387) were largely incorporated into the revenue omnibus LB 217 [18-22]. Additionally, he successfully advanced LB 137, adopting the Unclaimed Life Insurance Benefits Act [23, 24].
