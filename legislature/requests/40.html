What didn’t get passed that will probably be reintroduced next session?

2026-08-19 18:14:28 · Sessions 109B · standard

Nebraska Legislature 109th Legislature, 2nd Session

While many bills were indefinitely postponed or failed to advance during the session, the legislature’s interim study resolutions (LRs) provide the most direct evidence of which topics will likely be reintroduced next session. These resolutions designate specific committees to examine complex issues during the legislative break to prepare for future legislation.

Major Policy Areas for Potential Reintroduction

  • Medical Cannabis: Despite significant efforts through LB1235 and LB677, medical cannabis did not achieve final passage [1, 2]. However, the legislature has established interim studies (LR366 and LR367) to continue examining medical cannabis regulation and its potential implementation [109B-3].
  • Universal Child Care: A new effort for universal child care at no cost to parents (modeled after programs in New Mexico) will be examined under LR393 [109B-4]. This study aims to develop a model for potential legislation to address unmet needs and funding mechanisms in Nebraska [109B-4].
  • Divestment from the People’s Republic of China: Following the debate over LB1095, the legislature passed LR476 to study the fiscal and administrative implications of divesting state retirement system assets from China [109B-5]. This includes determining a "best timeline" for divestment in the context of national security [109B-5].
  • Portable Benefits for Independent Workers: The Banking, Commerce and Insurance Committee will use the interim to study portable benefits systems (LR414) [6, 7]. These frameworks would allow benefits like health coverage and retirement savings to follow independent contractors across multiple platforms and clients [109B-6].

Healthcare and Medicaid Reform

Several healthcare initiatives that faced obstacles are set for further study:

  • Medicaid Managed Care: The legislature will investigate adding long-term care services and supports to the Medicaid managed care program (LR481), following the introduction of LB832 [109B-8].
  • Developmental Disability Waivers: LR404 was introduced to examine assessment tools and recent eligibility requirements/caps placed on waivers by the Department of Health and Human Services [109B-9].
  • Olmstead Plan Evaluation: LB737, which required joint public hearings to evaluate the state's progress on the Olmstead plan for individuals with disabilities, will be a focal point of ongoing evaluation.

Education and Local Government

  • Exclusionary Discipline: Following debates on student behavior, LR466 proposes a study on reducing school reliance on exclusionary discipline (like suspensions) while maintaining safe learning environments [109B-11].
  • School Building Funds: LR406 will examine the special building funds that school boards can establish, specifically looking at current tax levy limits and how these funds are utilized [109B-12].
  • Residential Building Codes: Two studies (LR432 and LR441) will look at how current building and energy codes impact the cost of affordable housing and the rehabilitation of older properties [13, 14].
  • Municipal Board Burnout: LR419 will examine "volunteer burnout" on municipal boards and identify which commissions could function with fewer members [109B-15].

Placeholder "Shell Bills"

Several "shell bills" were introduced as placeholders and, if their intended purposes were not fulfilled, they are common candidates for reintroduction:

  • LB1105: Appropriations placeholder [109B-16].
  • LB1239: Health and Human Services placeholder [109B-17].
  • LB796: Pharmacy issue placeholder [109B-18].
  • LB648: Sales and use tax placeholder [109B-19].

Additionally, high-profile topics that were indefinitely postponed but remain recurring legislative priorities include E-Verify requirements for employers (LB532) and efforts to establish permanent standard time (LB302, LB34) [20-22].
