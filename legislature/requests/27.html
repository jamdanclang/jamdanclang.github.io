I want to introduce a bill that will establish rent control in urban areas with high housing costs. Has this ever been tried before? What current senators might be willing to collaborate with me?

2026-08-01 16:57:00 · Sessions 109B · deep

Establishing rent control in Nebraska is a contentious and evolving issue within the 109th Legislature. While broad private-sector rent control has not been successfully enacted, there are active efforts to both prohibit local governments from creating such mandates and to establish specific rent caps within public housing frameworks.

History and Current Status of Rent Control Efforts

The sources indicate that direct rent control on private property is currently facing significant legislative opposition, while targeted measures for public housing and indirect financial relief for tenants are being explored.

  • Proposed Prohibitions (2026): In the Second Session, LB809 was introduced by Senator Dover to explicitly strip local governments of the power to enact or enforce ordinances that "have the effect of imposing rent controls on private property" [1, 2]. The bill’s intent is to protect property owner rights and prevent local mandates for participation in federal housing assistance programs [3]. It does, however, provide exceptions for voluntary programs where a property owner contractually agrees to rent restrictions [2].
  • Public Housing Rent Caps (2026): Conversely, LB1045 (the Nebraska Public Housing Preservation Trust Act), introduced by Senator McKinney, proposes a specific rent control measure for public housing [4, 5]. For developments undergoing "preservation conversions" under this act, rent shall be capped at thirty percent of the resident's household income [6].
  • Source of Income Protections (2025): LB223, introduced by Senator Guereca, sought to prohibit housing discrimination based on a tenant's "lawful source of income," which includes federal housing assistance or vouchers [7-9]. This directly conflicts with the goals of LB809, which seeks to allow landlords to refuse such vouchers [2].
  • Indirect Relief and Affordability Measures: Other legislative efforts have focused on reducing the cost burden for renters through tax policy and supply expansion:
  • LB107 (2025): Proposed a refundable income tax credit for renters equal to $200 or 4% of total rent paid (capped at $1,000) to mirror property tax credits provided to homeowners [10-12].
  • LB1041 (2026): Requires cities and counties to allow accessory dwelling units (ADUs) on single-family lots to increase housing density and supply [13-15].
  • LB850 (2026): Authorizes metropolitan and primary class cities to use economic development programs for the construction or rehabilitation of housing [16-18].

Potential Collaborators

Based on recent bill introductions and committee leadership, several current senators are deeply engaged in housing affordability and tenant protections.

Lead Advocates for Tenant Rights and Public Affordability
  • Senator Terrell McKinney (District 11): As Chairperson of the Urban Affairs Committee, he is a central figure in housing reform [19]. He introduced the public housing rent cap bill (LB1045), the Residential Tenant Clean Slate Act (LB92) to remove eviction stigmas, and LB981 to mandate housing agency responsibilities regarding pest infestations [4, 20-22].
  • Senator Ashlei Spivey (District 13): A co-introducer of the public housing rent cap (LB1045), she also introduced LB164 (incentives for affordable commercial/residential development) and LB1156 (tax credits for disinvested community development) [4, 23, 24].
  • Senator John Cavanaugh (District 9): He introduced LB17, which targets "predatory" hidden fees in rental agreements, and LB850, which allows urban centers to use development funds for housing [16, 25, 26].
Advocates for Cost Reduction and Expanded Supply
  • Senator Victor Rountree (District 3): His focus is on supply and accessibility, having introduced LB1041 (ADUs) and LB840, which sets accessibility standards for multifamily housing projects receiving state aid [13, 27, 28].
  • Senator Machaela Cavanaugh (District 6): She introduced the renter’s tax credit (LB107) and LB1035, which seeks to limit year-over-year increases in homeowner insurance policies, indicating a broad interest in stabilizing residential costs [10, 29, 30].
  • Senator George Dungan (District 26): He has focused on lease transparency and legal rights, introducing LB1007 (protecting security deposits and prohibiting nondisclosure clauses) and LB101 (providing a right to jury trial in eviction cases) [31-33].
  • Senator Dunixi Guereca (District 7): He introduced protections against source-of-income discrimination (LB223) and the Nebraska Neighborhood Revitalization Act (LB620) to address nuisance properties and health risks [7, 34, 35].

Uncertainty and Strategic Considerations

The sources demonstrate a strong legislative divide. While there is a coalition of urban senators focused on tenant protections (McKinney, Spivey, J. Cavanaugh, Rountree), there is also a significant push by senators like Dover (LB809) and Kauth (LB834) to protect property owner rights and limit local regulatory power [1, 3, 36]. A bill establishing broad rent control in private urban areas would likely face intense scrutiny and potential preemption from the interests represented in LB809.
