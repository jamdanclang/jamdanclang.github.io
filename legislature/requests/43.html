Reconciling Conflicts and Distinguishing Sessions

The original question asks about Nebraska's actions with the federal CHIPS for America Act. The provided session research does not contain any information on this topic from the 108th, 107th, or 106th Legislatures.

However, it is mentioned that during the 105th Legislature (2018), there was no mention of the federal semiconductor CHIPS Act. Instead, legislative sources frequently address "CHIP" in the context of health insurance:

  • Children's Health Insurance Program (CHIP):
  • LB 985 proposed to mandate that if federal funding is no longer available to cover CHIP, the Nebraska Legislature must ensure sufficient state funds are appropriated to cover the program's costs [105B-1].
  • Comprehensive Health Insurance Pool (CHIP):
  • LB 550 (amending LB 241) was introduced to eliminate the requirement that the CHIP board utilize an independent actuary to establish rates, though the board could still choose to do so if necessary [105B-2].
  • Children's Health Care Coverage:
  • LB 702 was introduced and advanced by the Health and Human Services Committee to modify provisions relating to children's health care coverage [3, 4].

Specifically, under the Nebraska Transformational Projects Act (adopted via LB 1107), the state authorized up to \$300 million in matching funds from the Cash Reserve Fund for a postsecondary institution with a college of medicine [1-3].
  • However, the session's sources do address the federal Children's Health Insurance Program (CHIP). Under LB293, introduced in January 2019, Nebraska utilized the unused administrative cap allowed under CHIP [2-4]. Specifically, the Legislature directed Health and Human Services Cash Funds to serve as the state match for payments to the Nebraska Regional Poison Center, which is funded through the University of Nebraska Medical Center [3, 4]. This required state match was sourced from cash funds transferred directly from the University of Nebraska Medical Center [3, 4].
  • If the query refers to the federal Children's Health Insurance Program (CHIP), LB22 specifies that up to \$250,000 in cash funds may be allocated for state aid to match the unused administrative cap allowed under the program for payments to the Nebraska Regional Poison Center [105A-1].
