What were the bills passed in the 2022 legislative sessions that affected the Nebraska Department of Economic Development?

2026-08-05 18:53:18 · Sessions 107B · standard

Nebraska Legislature 107th Legislature, 2nd Session

Several bills passed during the 2022 legislative session significantly impacted the Nebraska Department of Economic Development (DED) through budget adjustments, the creation of new divisions and grant programs, and the modification of existing tax incentive acts.

New Divisions and Major Recovery Acts

  • LB1024 (Economic Recovery Act): This bill adopted the Economic Recovery Act and required the DED to create the Economic Recovery and Incentives Division [1, 2]. The department is tasked with developing a coordinated plan for economic recovery in areas of the state (primarily North and South Omaha) that were disproportionately impacted by the COVID-19 pandemic [3, 4].
  • LB450 (Nebraska Innovation Hub Act and Small Business Assistance Act): This legislation adopted the Nebraska Innovation Hub Act, requiring the DED to designate innovation hubs (iHubs) in economic redevelopment areas or enterprise zones to stimulate partnerships and job creation [5-7]. It also adopted the Small Business Assistance Act [8].

Grant Programs and Infrastructure Duties

  • LB1014 (ARPA Appropriations): This bill appropriated federal American Rescue Plan Act (ARPA) funds to the DED for several initiatives, including:
  • $20 million for wastewater pretreatment and runoff control for meat processing plant site development [9, 10].
  • $50 million for grants to capital projects eligible under the Shovel-Ready Capital Recovery and Investment Act [11].
  • Grants to nonprofit organizations to develop affordable housing for refugees and immigrants [12, 13].
  • LB598 (Small Business Stabilization Grant Program Act): This act created a grant program administered by the DED to provide financial assistance of up to $12,000 to eligible small businesses experiencing significant revenue loss due to a qualifying state of emergency [14, 15].
  • LB1099 (Nebraska Hydrogen Hub Industry Work Group): The DED was directed to create this work group to develop a competitive proposal for the state to be selected as a regional clean hydrogen hub by the U.S. Department of Energy [16-18].
  • LB998 (Municipal Inland Port Authority Act): This bill amended certification provisions and tasked the DED with prioritizing inland port authority proposals and promulgating rules for the Site and Building Development Act relating to these authorities [19, 20].

Workforce and Education

  • LB902 (Nebraska Career Scholarship Act): This act requires the DED to allocate scholarship funds to private nonprofit postsecondary institutions and establish a separate budget subprogram for these allocations [21-23].
  • LB1011 & LB1012 (Budget Adjustments): These bills modified funding and administration for:
  • The Nebraska Developing Youth Talent Initiative, requiring the DED to select industry partners and establish a separate budget subprogram [24, 25].
  • Internship programs, expanding eligibility and clarifying reimbursable expenses [26].

Tax Incentive and Business Act Modifications

  • LB1150 & LB1261: These bills modified major incentive acts administered by the DED:
  • ImagiNE Nebraska Act: Changed application and agreement procedures and redefined "base year" [27-29].
  • Nebraska Advantage Rural Development Act: Increased annual thresholds and credit caps while extending sunset dates [30, 31].
  • Business Innovation Act: Added definitions for economic redevelopment areas and included innovation hubs within the act’s scope [26, 32, 33].
  • Urban Redevelopment Act: Changed provisions regarding locations and taxpayer qualifications [29, 34].

Fund Administration

  • LB1012 and related amendments also impacted the Site and Building Development Fund, authorizing its use for a public-private-partnership facility at Offutt Air Force Base and for industrial site developments [26, 35, 36].
