What did LB1048 do?

2026-08-25 14:21:22 · Sessions 109B · standard

Nebraska Legislature 109th Legislature, 2nd Session

Legislative Bill 1048 (LB1048), introduced by Speaker John Arch at the request of the Governor, served as a major government modernization and reporting clean-up bill designed to streamline state administrative processes and eliminate redundant obligations [1, 2].

Specifically, LB1048 accomplished the following:

  • Eliminated and Consolidated Government Reports: It modified or outright eliminated numerous regular reporting requirements for several state entities, including the Departments of Agriculture, Labor, Revenue, Administrative Services, Correctional Services, Economic Development, Insurance, Transportation, and Water, Energy, and Environment, as well as the Board of Parole, a task force under the Commission on Law Enforcement and Criminal Justice, and broadband Internet providers under the Nebraska Broadband Bridge Act [1, 3]. For example, it adjusted the Department of Insurance's reporting frequency from annual to biennial [109B-4].
  • Abolished Administrative Boards: It outright abolished the Suggestion Award Board and the state employee suggestion system [1-3].
  • Strengthened Sole-Source and Emergency Contract Oversight: It mandated that copies of emergency and sole-source service contracts exceeding \$50,000, along with written state agency justifications, must be provided to the Director of Administrative Services and the Auditor of Public Accounts within three business days of approval [5, 6].
  • Established Parole and Release Reporting: It required the Board of Parole and the Department of Correctional Services to electronically submit an annual report documenting the percentage and characteristics of offenders who serve their entire sentence in a correctional facility and are released with no supervision, alongside recommendations to reduce this occurrence [7, 8].
  • Reformed State Institution Costs and County Payments: It eliminated provisions relating to county payments and the costs of care for patients residing in state institutions, such as the Beatrice State Developmental Center, Lincoln Regional Center, and Norfolk Regional Center [1, 3, 9, 10].
  • Restricted Juvenile Placement: It legally prohibited committed juvenile offenders from being placed, housed, or transferred to adult correctional facilities [109B-11].
  • Revised Budget and Labor Reports: It modified reporting procedures for preparing the state executive budget and eliminated reports concerning state labor negotiations [1, 3].
