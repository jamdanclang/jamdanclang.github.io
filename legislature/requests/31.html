What was the most divisive bill in the last 5 years?

2026-08-03 03:25:28 · Sessions 109B · standard

Nebraska Legislature 109th Legislature, 2nd Session

Based on the committee statements and legislative records provided, several bills from the 109th Legislature stand out as highly divisive due to split committee votes, massive numbers of testifiers in opposition, and procedural maneuvers like cloture or numerous floor amendments.

LB 3: Presidential Elector Selection

LB 3, which proposed changing Nebraska’s method of allocating presidential electors to a "winner-take-all" system, appears to be one of the most controversial bills [1].

  • Split Vote: The Government, Military and Veterans Affairs Committee advanced the bill with a narrow 5-3 vote [1].
  • Public Opposition: The committee statement lists an exceptionally large number of opponents (dozens of individuals and organizations) who provided testimony against the bill, compared to a relatively short list of proponents [1-3].

LB 730: Gender-Related and "Medical Conscience" Issues

Legislation introduced by Senator Kauth regarding gender-related regulations and "medical conscience" objections also showed deep division.

  • LB 730: Advanced from committee on another narrow 5-3 vote [4].
  • Extensive Testimony: Similar to LB 3, LB 730 drew a massive list of opponents—spanning two full pages of the committee statement—while proponents were significantly fewer [4-6].

LB 1071 & LB 1072: State Budget and Fund Transfers

The main appropriations and "housekeeping" bills for the state budget were centers of intense procedural conflict.

  • Floor Conflict: LB 1071 required a cloture motion (a move to end a filibuster) introduced by the Speaker to cease debate [7].
  • Volume of Amendments: LB 1072 was subject to a vast number of floor amendments and motions to strike sections, primarily from Senators Machaela Cavanaugh and Conrad, indicating a high level of disagreement over specific funding priorities [8-11].
  • Opponent Testimony: LB 1072 saw opposition from a wide array of interest groups, including conservationists, public employees, and policy institutes [12].

Other Highly Divisive Measures

Several other bills were advanced with significant internal committee disagreement or industry opposition:

  • LB 79 (Commercial Vehicle Negligence): This bill, which capped noneconomic damages and changed liability rules for commercial motor vehicle accidents, passed the Judiciary Committee on a 5-3 vote [13, 14].
  • LB 656 (SNAP Work Requirements): Advanced on a 4-3 vote, with significant opposition from advocacy groups for the poor and tribal representatives [15].
  • LB 169 (Sales Tax Exemptions): This bill drew opposition from a broad coalition of industries (theaters, zoos, tattooists, and the Chamber of Commerce) because it sought to eliminate numerous long-standing sales tax exemptions [16, 17].
  • LB 468 (Inheritance Tax/Data Centers): Advanced on a 6-2 vote and required cloture to move forward [18, 19].

While the sources cover the 109th Legislature (2025-2026), LB 3 and LB 730 stand out as particularly divisive due to the volume of public participation and the consistently split votes of the committees overseeing them.
