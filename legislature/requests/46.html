What groups got special recognition during the legislative sessions?

2026-08-22 18:36:11 · Sessions 109B,109A,108B,108A · standard

Special Recognition Groups During Legislative Sessions

109th Legislature, 2nd Session

Several groups received special recognition through commemorative measures, specialty license plates, and educational mandates:

  • Veterans and Military:
  • Honored with "Honoring Women Veterans" license plates (beginning 2027) [1, 2]
  • Had thirteen "Military Honor Plates" designs [3, 4]
  • Introduced Space Force plates [109B-5]
  • Authorized a Capitol plaque honoring Medal of Honor recipients [109B-6]
  • Native Americans:
  • Established "Native American Cultural Awareness and History Plates" [109B-7]
  • Mandated "Native American Heritage Day" school exercises [8, 9]
  • Funded a museum honoring Chief Standing Bear [109B-10]
  • Law Enforcement and Ancestry:
  • Authorized "Back the Blue" plates [109B-11]
  • Authorized "Czech Heritage" plates [109B-12]
  • Commemorative Months (2026 Resolutions):
  • Designated months honored Firefighters (LR307) [109B-13]
  • Honored Nuclear Power employees (LR329) [109B-14]
  • Recognized African Americans (LR322) [109B-15]
  • Honored Women (LR348) [109B-16]
  • Recognized Multiple Sclerosis patients (LR344) [109B-17]
  • Failed Proposals:
  • Mandated a model policy addressing antisemitism [109B-18]
  • Proposed expanding protections and recognition to encompass "anti-blackness" and "anti-indigenous" discrimination, but did not pass [109B-19]
109th Legislature, 1st Session

Veterans, Servicemembers, and Spouses received special recognition through enacted legislation and proposed bills:

  • Veterans, Servicemembers, and Spouses:
  • Expanded veterans' preference in public and private employment [1, 2]
  • Recognized military service with retailored license plates including the U.S. Space Force and Inherent Resolve Campaign Medal [3-5]
  • Accelerated a "veteran justice program" utilizing deferred judgments to become operative on July 1, 2025 [6-9]
  • African Americans:
  • Restructured the Commission on African American Affairs, mandating quarterly meetings in the state's largest African American population center to address disparities [12, 13]
108th Legislature, 2nd Session

The Nebraska Legislature recognized several key groups through legislative resolutions:

  • Military and Veterans:
  • Honored the American Legion on its 105th anniversary for over a century of veteran advocacy via LR309 [1, 2]
  • Congratulated the U.S. Air Force 45th Reconnaissance Squadron "Wildcats" at Offutt Air Force Base on its 30th anniversary under LR302 [3, 4]
  • Civic and Historical Organizations:
  • Commended the National Conference of State Legislatures for its 50 years of service via LR300 [5, 6]
  • Congratulated the National Society of Pershing Rifles on its 130th anniversary under LR446 [7, 8]
  • Athletic and Student Groups:
  • Celebrated the Omaha Supernovas for their inaugural professional volleyball season under LR474 [9, 11]
108th Legislature, 1st Session

The legislative sources for this session do not contain resolutions or bills dedicated to granting "special recognition" to specific groups.

Additional Cited Findings

  • Veterans, Servicemembers, and Spouses:
  • LB144 (approved April 9, 2025) expanded veterans' preference in public and private employment [1, 2]
  • LB134 (also incorporated in LB398) recognized military service with retailored license plates including the U.S. Space Force and Inherent Resolve Campaign Medal [3-5]
  • LB150 (amended May 2025) accelerated a "veteran justice program" utilizing deferred judgments to become operative on July 1, 2025 [6-9]
  • African Americans:
  • LB69 restructured the Commission on African American Affairs, mandating quarterly meetings in the state's largest African American population center to address disparities [12, 13]
  • The proposal for a Ghana exchange program under LB703 remained only introduced [14, 15]
  • First Responders:
  • LB608 expanded the First Responder Recruitment and Retention Act to include correctional and youth detention officers [109A-16]

Additional cited findings

  • However, the provided documents highlight advocacy and legislative proposals concerning specific demographics: - People of Washington, D.C.: LR146, introduced by Senator Danielle Conrad, urges the United States Congress to enact federal legislation granting statehood to the people of Washington, D.C.
