Is there a prison overpopulation problem in Nebraska?

2026-07-31 01:03:17 · Sessions 109B · standard

Nebraska Legislature 109th Legislature, 2nd Session

Based on the legislative documents provided, Nebraska recognizes a significant prison overcrowding problem, as evidenced by specific statutes, dedicated funds, and active construction projects to expand facility capacity.

The sources highlight several key indicators of this issue:

1. Statutory Definition of an Overcrowding Emergency

Nebraska law specifically defines a "correctional system overcrowding emergency" as occurring when the Director of Correctional Services certifies that the inmate population exceeds 140% of operational design capacity [1, 2]. When such an emergency is in effect, the Board of Parole is required to immediately consider or reconsider eligible committed offenders for release [1-3].

2. Dedicated Overcrowding Funds

The Legislature established the Prison Overcrowding Contingency Fund, which received a $15 million transfer from the General Fund [4]. These funds are intended to remain in place until the Department of Correctional Services provides "sufficient details... regarding plans to reduce prison overcrowding" [4]. Additionally, the Department maintains a Prison Overcrowding Contingency Cash Fund [5-7].

3. Construction of a New Facility

To address capacity needs, the Department of Correctional Services has been authorized to design and construct a new multi-custody-level correctional facility [8-10].

  • Capacity: The new facility is designed to house approximately 1,512 inmates [8, 11].
  • Name: This facility, located at 9440 North 70th Street in Lincoln, has been named the Arbor Correctional Center [12, 13].
  • Purpose: The construction is part of a plan that includes the eventual decommissioning of the Nebraska State Penitentiary [14-16].

4. Required Reporting

The Department of Correctional Services and the Board of Parole must submit annual reports during years they operate under an overcrowding emergency [17, 18]. These reports must evaluate the success of parolees granted "controlled release" specifically due to the Correctional System Overcrowding Emergency Act [17, 19, 20]. Legislators have also requested detailed timelines for decommissioning old facilities and transitioning to the new 1,512-bed facility to ensure these projects effectively manage the population [14, 21, 22].
