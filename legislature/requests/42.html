How did the budget bills lead to the current budget deficit?

2026-08-21 00:05:10 · Sessions 109B,109A,108B,108A,107B,107A,106B,106A · standard

The Nebraska Legislature's budget bills did not directly lead to the current budget deficit.

While the provided sources detail specific deficits and funding shortages within certain state programs, such as the Division of Children and Family Services Deficit [109B-1] and General Fund unavailability for housing programs [109B-2], they do not explain how these budget bills contributed to a general state budget deficit.

The 2024 Special Session's elimination of $40 million in DHHS funding is directly linked to the Division of Children and Family Services Deficit, but it does not imply that this action led to the current budget deficit.

Instead, the sources highlight broader funding shortages across other state programs and acknowledge the existence of an estimated budget shortfall [109A-1]. To address this shortfall, Legislative Bill 650 (LB650) proposes to sunset several tax credits and income reductions, eliminate certain sales and use tax exemptions, and alter the classification of out-of-state income [109A-1].

The sources also mention employee furloughs as a short-term method to manage "budgetary shortfalls" [2-4], but they do not explain how these measures contributed to the current budget deficit.

In addition, the documents outline various legislative proposals designed to address revenue gaps and generate additional state revenue, such as introducing LB 1067 to increase the documentary stamp tax and redirect those proceeds directly to workforce housing funds [2-4].

The sources also detail mid-biennium adjustments and cash fund sweeps to manage the state's primary budget and support proposed reductions in spending [7, 8].

However, these measures do not imply that they led to the current budget deficit.

In contrast, the 107th Legislature's handling of budgetary shortfalls with furloughs [1-3] and managing General Fund adequacy via transfers [4, 5] does not explain how these actions contributed to a general state budget deficit.

The available materials cover the appropriations, budget adjustments, and fund transfers enacted during the 106th Legislature (primarily the 2nd Session in 2020 and 1st Session in 2019) but do not mention a current budget deficit.

In summary, the provided sources do not contain information explaining how Nebraska's budget bills led to a current budget deficit.

Additional cited findings:

  • Handling Budgetary Shortfalls with Furloughs: Under budget bills like LB294, state agencies were given the discretionary authority to utilize employee furloughs (placing employees in temporary, nonduty, nonpay status) as a short-term mechanism to address "budgetary shortfalls" [1-3].
  • Managing General Fund Adequacy via Transfers: Rather than operating in a deficit, the State Treasurer was repeatedly authorized to transfer substantial sums from the Cash Reserve Fund to the General Fund when the General Fund cash balance was inadequate to meet current obligations [4, 5].
  • Mid-Biennium Adjustments and Cash Fund Sweeps: The Legislature utilized mid-biennium budget adjustments to actively reallocate appropriations for state operations, aid, and capital construction in response to changing economic conditions and updated revenue forecasts [11, 12].
  • Fund Transfers from the General Fund: The available materials detail major fund transfers from the General Fund, including transferring $272,000,000 to the Property Tax Credit Cash Fund in both December 2019 and December 2020, alongside transfers to the Water Sustainability Fund and the Water Resources Cash Fund [106A-7].
  • Cash Reserve Fund Safeguards: To prevent financial instability, the Legislature established strict rules requiring the Tax Commissioner to monitor the Cash Reserve Fund balance [7, 9].
