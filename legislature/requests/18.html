What does LB1064 do?

2026-07-25 02:04:04 · Sessions 109B · standard

Nebraska Legislature 109th Legislature, 2nd Session

LB1064, introduced by Senator Bostar, adopts the Large Load Customer Regulation Act [1-4]. The primary purpose of the act is to regulate retail load customers requesting new or expanded interconnections where the total load at a single site would exceed twenty megawatts (20MW) [3, 5-7].

The provisions of LB1064 were eventually incorporated into LB1010 through the Standing Committee Amendment AM2422 [3, 7].

Key Provisions of LB1064

According to the sources, the bill and its subsequent amendments accomplish the following:

  • Interconnection Standards: Public power suppliers must establish standards for interconnecting large load customers to support business development while maintaining system reliability and mitigating the potential for stranded infrastructure costs [3, 5, 6].
  • Mandatory Disclosures: Large load customers are required to disclose:
  • Whether they are pursuing a substantially similar request for electric service elsewhere that could result in them materially changing or withdrawing their current request [3, 5-7].
  • Information regarding their onsite backup generating facilities [5, 8, 9].
  • Study Fees: For initial studies for loads exceeding 20MW, customers must pay a study fee [7, 8, 10]. While the original bill set this fee at a flat rate of at least $100,000, the committee amendment (AM2392) adjusted it to $50,000 or $1,000 per megawatt, whichever is greater [7, 9, 11]. The public power supplier is required to complete the study within one year of receiving the fee [7, 10, 11].
  • Site Control and Financial Commitment: Customers must provide a method to demonstrate site control for the proposed load location and meet financial commitment requirements for the development of the transmission and generation infrastructure needed to serve them [7-10].
  • Negotiated Rates: Public power suppliers are authorized to negotiate or establish rates and operating standards for large load customers that fairly allocate electricity system costs and mitigate financial, operational, and resource adequacy risks to other customers [7, 8, 10]. They are not required to establish a general rate tariff for these customers [7, 8, 10].
  • Emergency Procedures: Public power suppliers must develop procedures to procure demand response and load flexibility from large load customers [8, 10, 12]. In the event of an emergency condition or grid instability, affected large load customers may be required to curtail their load or deploy their onsite backup generating facilities [9, 10, 12, 13].
