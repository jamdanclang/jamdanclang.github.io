Does Nebraska have a requirement of a balanced budget?

2026-07-31 02:10:11 · Sessions 109B · standard

Nebraska Legislature 109th Legislature, 2nd Session

The sources do not explicitly quote a single constitutional or statutory provision that uses the exact phrase "balanced budget requirement." However, they describe a state fiscal framework and specific procedures that mandate the balancing of estimated revenues with proposed expenditures at both the state and local levels.

State-Level Budgeting Requirements

For the state government, the budget process involves several mandatory steps to ensure expenditures do not exceed revenues:

  • Governor’s Duty to Present a Complete Budget: The Governor is required by law to present a "complete budget" to the Legislature by January 15 of each odd-numbered year (or February 1 in their first year) [1]. This budget must be a tentative program for the coming biennium and must contain:
  • An itemized report of expenditures from the previous Legislature's appropriations [1].
  • Estimated revenue from taxation and other sources [1].
  • An estimate of the amount required to be raised by taxation and the specific sales and income tax rates necessary to raise that amount [1].
  • Constitutional Authority: The Legislature acknowledges its responsibility under Article III, section 22, of the Constitution of Nebraska, which provides that each Legislature shall make appropriations for the expenses of the government [2, 3].
  • Use of the Cash Reserve Fund: To manage potential shortfalls, Nebraska maintains a Cash Reserve Fund [4, 5]. The State Treasurer is mandated to transfer funds from this reserve to the General Fund if the Director of Administrative Services certifies that the General Fund balance is "inadequate to meet current obligations" [4, 5]. Such transfers must be reversed once sufficient funds become available in the General Fund [4, 5].

Local-Level Budgeting Requirements

The Nebraska Budget Act (Sections 13-501 to 13-513) governs the financial affairs of political subdivisions, including counties, cities, and villages [6-8].

  • Adopted Budget Statements: Governing bodies must adopt budget statements that define their fiscal year's financial and taxing affairs [7, 9].
  • Public Access to Financial Data: To ensure transparency, the Department of Administrative Services is tasked with developing a database of financial information for all counties, cities, and villages, including revenue sources, expenditures, and balance sheets showing all assets and liabilities [6, 8, 10].

Debt Limitations

The state's ability to borrow is also restricted. For instance, in specific investment programs, obligations issued are "special, limited obligation[s]" payable solely from program revenues and "shall not constitute a general obligation of the State of Nebraska or a pledge of the full faith and credit of the State of Nebraska" [11, 12]. These programs are also required to maintain a debt service coverage ratio consistent with prudent fiscal standards [11, 12].
