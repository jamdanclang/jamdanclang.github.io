If I was a left-leaning politician, who might I pattern my career off of? Are there any bills that they failed to enact that I might pick up next session?

2026-08-03 02:59:01 · Sessions 109B,109A,108B,108A,107B,107A,106B,106A,105B,105A · deep

Cross-session synthesis

Bottom line

The strongest model is not one senator but a hybrid career: Danielle Conrad's legal and institutional rigor, Machaela Cavanaugh's persistence on family economic security, Megan Hunt's civil-liberties and coalition work, and the long-view justice advocacy of Ernie Chambers and Patty Pansing Brooks. Across 2017-2026, the clearest pattern is durable issue ownership: senators repeatedly reintroduced, refined, or handed off the same progressive ideas until the coalition or political moment changed.

Career models worth studying

  • Danielle Conrad offers the broadest current template: civil liberties, worker and family benefits, public accountability, and technically detailed legislation.
  • Machaela Cavanaugh is the clearest model for sustained campaigning around paid leave, school meals, renters, and the social safety net - even when a bill requires several sessions.
  • Megan Hunt combines LGBTQ+ rights, food security, labor policy, and public-facing advocacy. Her record is especially useful for learning how to keep minority-position issues visible.
  • Ernie Chambers and Patty Pansing Brooks provide the strongest justice-reform lineage, spanning the death penalty, cash bail, sentencing, juvenile confinement, sexual-assault survivor rights, and civil rights.
  • Tony Vargas, Adam Morfeld, Matt Hansen, and Terrell McKinney are useful models for pairing constituency-based advocacy with concrete economic, housing, anti-discrimination, and racial-equity proposals.

Best ideas to pick up in the 110th Legislature

1. Paid family and medical leave is the most obvious multi-session project. Versions appeared as LB305, LB311, LB290, and most recently LB189. The repetition signals both durable demand and an existing policy/sponsor network. 2. Worker-pay reform, especially the tipped minimum wage, recurred through LB211, LB400, and LB122. A new version could be paired with Conrad's worker-benefit and tax-credit work. 3. LGBTQ+ nondiscrimination and conversion-therapy restrictions persisted through LB173, LB627, LB120, LB167, and later proposals. This is another mature policy lineage with prior language and testimony to reuse. 4. Housing security has several actionable entry points: the renter credit in LB107, eviction-record sealing in LB128, and earlier tenant-protection work. This area may offer more incremental paths than a single omnibus bill. 5. Criminal-justice reform could build from cash-bail reform (LB646), sentencing reform (LB447/LB131), juvenile-confinement limits (LB230), and solitary-confinement reform (LB99). 6. Food security has a long thread from SNAP eligibility and income-limit bills to Hunger-Free Schools proposals. The school-meals campaign is particularly instructive because it shows how an idea can be refined across multiple sessions. 7. Climate policy is less consistently represented in these briefs, but Tony Vargas's LB283 climate-action-plan proposal is a clear dormant measure worth reassessing.

Practical career lesson

Choose two lanes: one achievable, incremental lane where you can demonstrate enacted results, and one long-horizon lane that you are willing to carry for years. Build from prior bill text, hearing records, fiscal notes, and sponsor coalitions instead of starting over. The historical record suggests that persistence and handoffs across senators matter more than attaching a career to one personality.

Important next-session caveat

The 110th Legislature is a new biennium. Nebraska defines carry-over legislation as measures introduced in an odd-numbered year and held into the even-numbered session of the same Legislature. That means unfinished 109th Legislature proposals generally need to be reintroduced in 2027 and may receive new bill numbers; current text, sponsors, fiscal notes, and legal conditions should be rechecked before filing. See the official Nebraska Legislature glossary cited below.

Session-by-session research briefs

Nebraska Legislature 109th Legislature, 2nd Session

Senator Machaela Cavanaugh (District 6) serves as a primary model for left-leaning legislative careers, championing worker protections and the social safety net [1, 2]. In the 109th Legislature, she introduced the Paid Family and Medical Leave Insurance Act (LB189) [1] and the Hunger-Free Schools Act (LB966) to establish universal school meal programs [3].

Failed proposals to pick up in future sessions include LB107, which offered an income tax credit for renters [4], and LB1153, which sought to redirect 96% of the State Settlement Cash Fund to common schools and pediatric research [5]. LB772 also failed to enact its goal of prohibiting transfers from multiple cash funds to the General Fund to protect multi-year projects [6]. Additionally, LB99, introduced by Senator Ashlei Spivey, aimed to reform the prison system by limiting solitary confinement to 15 consecutive days and prohibiting non-consensual double-bunking [7, 8]. These bills represent core progressive priorities in labor, criminal justice, and housing that remain unfinished [1, 4, 8].

Nebraska Legislature 109th Legislature, 1st Session

A left-leaning politician could pattern their career off Senator Danielle Conrad (District 46) or Senator Machaela Cavanaugh (District 6). Conrad focused on civil rights and worker protections, introducing LB493 (Uniform Public Expression Protection Act) [1, 2], LB156 to allow tort claims for child sexual assault in schools [3, 4], and LB588 to link ADC payments to inflation [5]. Cavanaugh prioritized social safety nets, proposing LB189 (Paid Family and Medical Leave) [6, 7] and LB14 (Hunger-Free Schools) to provide universal free meals [8, 9].

Proposals that did not pass this session—which could be reintroduced—include Conrad's LB157, a refundable Child Tax Credit [10], and LB30, which would exempt overtime pay from state income tax [11, 12]. Cavanaugh's LB53, providing criminal and civil immunity for pregnancy outcomes [13, 14], and LB107, a refundable income tax credit for renters [15, 16], also remain available. Additionally, Senator Terrell McKinney’s LB487, establishing a Redlining Task Force [17, 18], offers a blueprint for progressive economic redress.

Nebraska Legislature 108th Legislature, 2nd Session

Senator Danielle Conrad (District 46) serves as a primary model for left-leaning legislative focus, championing civil liberties, education equity, and social welfare [1-4]. Senator Machaela Cavanaugh (District 6) similarly prioritizes institutional accountability, notably introducing LR335 in March 2024 to censure a colleague for harassment [5, 6].

Future sessions could see the reintroduction of several failed or unfinished proposals:

  • LB1081: Establishing student-to-teacher ratios, currently an interim study under LR319.
  • LB864: Restoring stable funding for court interpreters following a 2023 gubernatorial veto of appropriated rate increases [7, 8].
  • LB366: Mandating the release of body-worn camera footage in custody deaths, now studied via LR426 [4].
  • LR5: Ratification of the 1924 federal Child Labor Amendment [9, 10].
  • LB1072: Incentivizing sustainable aviation fuel, under review in LR411 [11].
  • LR365: Addressing LGBTQ bullying prevention guidelines [12].
  • LR333: Implementing "Hunger-Free Campus" legislation to address student food and basic needs insecurity [3, 13].

Nebraska Legislature 108th Legislature, 1st Session

A left-leaning politician could pattern their career after Senator Carol Blood or Senator Danielle Conrad, both of whom championed progressive resolutions during the 108th Legislature [1, 2].

Senator Carol Blood introduced LR5 (hearing March 03, 2023), seeking to ratify the 1924 federal Child Labor Amendment [2]. Blood aimed to "fix this historical wrong" and make Nebraska a leader in the anti-child labor movement; however, the amendment still requires ten additional states for ratification [2].

Senator Danielle Conrad introduced LR146 (hearing February 21, 2024), which urges Congress to grant statehood to Washington, D.C. [1].

These proposals represent key initiatives to pick up in a future session. They stand in contrast to other session priorities, such as LR21, which moved to create a Small Modular Nuclear Reactor Study Committee [3, 4], and LR31, an application for a convention of states to limit federal power [5]. Reintroducing LR5 or LR146 would allow a successor to build on the legislative focus on labor and representation established by Blood and Conrad [1, 2].

Nebraska Legislature 107th Legislature, 2nd Session

For a career in left-leaning advocacy, Senator Megan Hunt (District 8) and Senator Machaela Cavanaugh (District 6) are primary models. Hunt championed reproductive rights, LGBTQ+ protections, and SNAP expansion [1-3]. Cavanaugh prioritized paid leave and hunger-free schools [4, 5].

Key failed proposals for future consideration include:

  • LB121 (Hunt): Restoring SNAP eligibility for individuals with past drug convictions [3]. Advanced February 17, 2021, but stalled after hostile floor amendments sought to strike the enacting clause [6, 7].
  • LB290 (Cavanaugh): The Paid Family and Medical Leave Insurance Act [5]. Advanced from committee February 8, 2021, but failed to pass the Legislature.
  • LB88 (Morfeld): Protecting student journalists' free speech rights. Advanced January 29, 2021, but was not enacted.
  • LB814 (McKinney): Requiring racial impact statements for legislation [9].
  • LB120 (Hunt): Prohibiting discrimination based on sexual orientation or gender identity [2].

These measures identify core policy gaps—economic security and systemic equity—that remained unresolved during this legislative session.

Nebraska Legislature 107th Legislature, 1st Session

Ernie Chambers is cited in LR4 as a model for "honest, fearless, selfless, intelligent, and informed public service" [1, 2]. He championed ending the death penalty and established the Nebraska Indian Child Welfare Act [1]. Contemporary progressive leaders include Megan Hunt, who introduced LB120 to prohibit LGBTQ+ employment discrimination and LB122 to eliminate the $2.13 tipped minimum wage [3-6]. Machaela Cavanaugh proposed the Hunger-Free Schools Act (LB117) to ensure all students receive no-cost meals [7, 8]. John McCollister introduced the Clean Slate Act (LB114) and LB125 for ranked-choice voting [9-12].

Significant proposals that failed this session and could be re-introduced include:

  • LB69: Student Loan Repayment Tax Credit Act (Jen Day); indefinitely postponed March 2, 2021 [13].
  • LB122: Eliminating the tipped minimum wage [4, 6].
  • LB120: Prohibiting discrimination based on sexual orientation and gender identity [3, 5].
  • LB128: Residential Tenant Clean Slate Act, which sought to seal eviction records [14, 15].
  • LR130: Commending federal action to revoke the Keystone XL Pipeline permit [16, 17].

Nebraska Legislature 106th Legislature, 2nd Session

In the 106th session, a left-leaning career might be patterned after Patty Pansing Brooks (District 28), who prioritized justice reform and civil rights. Her successful enactments include LB230 (passed 2020), which established strict requirements for juvenile room confinement [1, 2]. Failed proposals for next session include LB627, which sought to prohibit workplace discrimination based on sexual orientation and gender identity [3, 4], and LB848, which aimed to replace Columbus Day with Indigenous Peoples' Day [5, 6].

Alternatively, Megan Hunt (District 8) focused on labor and social equity. She failed to enact LB167, prohibiting conversion therapy [7], and LB400, which would have raised the minimum wage for tipped workers [8, 9]. Other vital carries include Ernie Chambers' LB646 to eliminate cash bail [10] and Tony Vargas' LB283, which required a state climate change action plan [11, 12]. Finally, Machaela Cavanaugh (District 6) championed LB1060, which reached Final Reading, to expand racial protections by including hair textures and protective hairstyles [13-15].

Nebraska Legislature 106th Legislature, 1st Session

Left-leaning politicians might pattern their careers after Patty Pansing Brooks (District 28), Matt Hansen (District 26), or Megan Hunt (District 8), who championed civil rights, labor protections, and criminal justice reform [1-4]. Pansing Brooks successfully enacted LB43 (2020), the Sexual Assault Survivors' Bill of Rights [5, 6], and LB230 (2020), which restricted juvenile room confinement [7, 8]. Matt Hansen successfully passed LB433 (2019), strengthening tenant protections regarding security deposits [9, 10].

Progressive proposals that failed to pass include:

  • LB627: A bill by Pansing Brooks to prohibit employment discrimination based on sexual orientation or gender identity [1, 4].
  • LB311: Senator Sue Crawford’s Paid Family and Medical Leave Insurance Act, designed to provide partial wage replacement for health or family needs [11, 12].
  • LB110: The Medical Cannabis Act, introduced by Senator Anna Wishart to establish a framework for medical access [13, 14].
  • LB167: Senator Hunt’s proposal to ban conversion therapy for minors [15, 16].
  • LB131: A Pansing Brooks proposal to reform minimum sentencing terms [17, 18].

Nebraska Legislature 105th Legislature, 2nd Session

A left-leaning politician could pattern their career after Adam Morfeld (District 46) or Tony Vargas (District 7), who focused on civil rights, consumer protections, and progressive taxation. Morfeld introduced LB173 (2017) to prohibit discrimination based on sexual orientation and gender identity [1, 2]. He also proposed the Internet Neutrality Act (LB856) in 2018 to prevent paid prioritization and protect freedom of information [3, 4].

Tony Vargas championed LB1074 (2018), which sought to create higher tax brackets for top earners to fund relief for low-income residents [5, 6]. He also introduced LB1082 (2018) to require public notice before local law enforcement enters federal immigration enforcement agreements [7, 8].

Other progressive proposals that failed to pass include Matt Hansen’s LB211 (2017), which aimed to raise the tipped minimum wage [9, 10], and Patty Pansing Brooks’ LB988 (2018), which proposed an affirmative consent "yes means yes" standard for sexual assault [11]. These bills remain available for introduction in future sessions.

Nebraska Legislature 105th Legislature, 1st Session

For a career modeled on left-leaning priorities, pattern your trajectory after Tony Vargas (District 7) or Patty Pansing Brooks (District 28). Vargas successfully enacted LB427 (2017) to establish breastfeeding rights in schools [1, 2] and LB194 (2018) to reform payday lending [3, 4]. Pansing Brooks passed LB289 (2017) to combat human trafficking [5, 6] and introduced LR153 to study the risks of conversion therapy [7, 8].

Several progressive proposals failed to pass this session and remain available for next session:

  • LB173 (Morfeld/Pansing Brooks): Prohibiting employment discrimination based on sexual orientation or gender identity [9, 10].
  • LB211 (Hansen): Raising the tipped minimum wage to 50% of the standard rate [11, 12].
  • LB305 (Crawford): Adopting the Paid Family Medical Leave Insurance Act [13].
  • LB447 (Chambers): Eliminating mandatory minimums for Class IC and ID felonies [14-16].
  • LB311 (Morfeld): Restoring SNAP eligibility for individuals with past drug-related convictions [17].
  • LB358 (McCollister): Expanding Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP) income limits [18].

Vargas also introduced LR26 (2017) to formally oppose federal efforts to rescind DACA [19, 20].
