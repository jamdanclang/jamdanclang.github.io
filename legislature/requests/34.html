Nebraska Legislature 109th Legislature, 2nd Session

Legislation in the 109th Legislature, 2nd Session, specifically targeted North Omaha through investment tools and housing reforms. LB1114 (Approved April 16, 2026) included AM2838, allowing inland port authorities to issue "community revenue bonds" with a 10% target allocation for qualified community investors to facilitate local investment [1-4]. This omnibus bill also incorporated LB981, mandating that metropolitan housing agencies conduct bed bug inspections and provide professional remediation [5-7].

Introduced proposals targeting "Qualified Census Tracts" (QCTs) included LB856, the Community Reinvestment and Equity Act, to establish an excise tax on certain businesses for reinvestment [8, 9], and LB1214, a neighborhood data collaborative pilot [10]. LB1045 proposed the Nebraska Public Housing Preservation Trust Act [11, 12].

Interim studies like LR407 were introduced to examine cultural capital designations for urban revitalization [13]. Finally, LR313 recognized the legacy of North Omaha civil rights leader Daniel Goodwin Sr. [14], and LR320 celebrated the Omaha North High girls basketball team [15].

Nebraska Legislature 109th Legislature, 1st Session

In the 109th Session, legislation shaped North Omaha by addressing historical systemic harm and targeted economic needs. LB487, the proposed Restitution and Redress for Redlining Task Force Act, identified North Omaha as a victim of 1930s "hazardous" lending designations and sought to study reparations for ongoing structural discrimination [1-3]. LB290 proposed requiring additional economic recovery grants be directed toward Qualified Census Tracts (QCTs) within metropolitan inland port districts [4].

Key outcomes included the passage of LB48, which established a five-year pilot program for Family Resource and Juvenile Assessment Centers in metropolitan areas to provide culturally relevant support for at-risk youth [5-7]. Further, LB69 was enacted (May 2025) to revitalize the Commission on African American Affairs, mandating annual meetings in Omaha—the city with the largest African American population—to evaluate proactive solutions for housing and economic disparities [8-10]. Other efforts, such as LB164, proposed new incentives for developers to build affordable commercial space in QCTs [11, 12].

Nebraska Legislature 108th Legislature, 2nd Session

The 108th Legislature shaped North Omaha primarily through economic recognition and targeted interim studies addressing historical disparities. LR306 (2024) celebrated the impact of North End Teleservices, an African-American-owned business founded for job creation in northeast Omaha; it has injected over $250 million into the economy and launched Forever North, representing the largest private investment in the area's history [1].

Senator McKinney (District 11) introduced several critical interim studies to reform local systems. LR439 and LR440 (2024) investigate the educational achievement gap and student underperformance within the Class V school district (Omaha Public Schools) [2, 3]. Additionally, LR341 was proposed to examine school "ban and bar" policies that can restrict parental involvement [4].

Regarding infrastructure and housing, LR360 was introduced to evaluate the effectiveness of the Middle Income Workforce Housing Investment Act, alongside LR359's review of property development concepts [5, 6]. Youth achievements were also codified, notably LR326, honoring Omaha North High School's first female individual state wrestling champion in 2024 [7].

Nebraska Legislature 107th Legislature, 2nd Session

During the 2022 session, the Economic Recovery Act (LB1024) emerged as the primary vehicle for shaping North Omaha, specifically addressing generational social and economic challenges fueled by historical redlining [1, 2]. The Act intended to appropriate $450 million in federal ARPA funds for housing, job training, and business development within qualified census tracts [3, 4]. Key outcomes included up to $60 million for a business park and $30 million for innovation hubs to stimulate tech-driven entrepreneurship [3, 5, 6].

LB927 further shaped the area by directing 10% of convention center support funds to high-poverty zones for reducing street violence and fostering small business growth [7-9]. Healthcare access was prioritized in LB1011, which provided specific state aid and minority health initiative funding to North Omaha’s Charles Drew Health Center [10-12]. Cultural legislation included LB1205, which advanced to establish the Ernie Chambers History-Arts-Humanities Museum [13, 14]. Additionally, LR325 recognized the Malcolm X Memorial Foundation for its contributions to community education and economic empowerment [15, 16].

Nebraska Legislature 107th Legislature, 1st Session

During the 107th Legislature, 1st Session, legislation shaped North Omaha through targeted economic incentives and social investments. LB 39 (Approved 5/25/21) significantly impacted the area by requiring that 10% of Convention Center Support Funds allocated to Omaha be distributed to "areas with a high concentration of poverty" (defined by census tracts with >30% poverty) to showcase historical aspects or assist in reducing street and gang violence [1-3].

LB 25 (Approved 5/26/21) implemented a constitutional amendment extending the tax-increment financing (TIF) period from 15 to 20 years for "extremely blighted areas," a designation used to spur development and workforce housing in distressed neighborhoods [4-6]. LR 4 honored Senator Ernie Chambers (District 11) upon his retirement, noting his legacy in establishing the Learning Community to eliminate educational disparities in North Omaha [7]. Interim studies further addressed local issues: LR 172 examined how nonprofit "oversaturation" affects economic growth in high-poverty areas [8], and LR 210 investigated the links between urban poverty and incarceration rates [9].

Nebraska Legislature 106th Legislature, 2nd Session

During the 106th Legislature, 2nd Session, legislation targeted North Omaha primarily through economic revitalization and housing reform. The significant outcome was LB 1107 (passed August 2020), which adopted the ImagiNE Small Business and Urban Revitalization Act [1-3]. This act provides tax incentives specifically for "economic redevelopment areas" characterized by high unemployment and a poverty rate of at least 20% [1, 4].

To address residential instability, LB 866 (amended by AM 2913) established the Municipal Density and Missing Middle Housing Act, requiring Omaha to create affordable housing action plans to counter the historic impact of redlining [5, 6]. Similarly, LB 1155 adopted the Middle Income Workforce Housing Investment Act, transferring $10 million to support housing in urban neighborhoods [7-9].

Proposals to increase equity in state contracting, such as LB 1218 (the Historically Underutilized Business Program Act), advanced to General File but did not pass [10, 11]. Justice reforms included LB 924, mandating implicit bias training for law enforcement, and the introduction of LB 1222 for municipal police oversight [12, 13]. Additionally, LR 374 initiated an interim study on the legal and social impacts of redlining [14].

Nebraska Legislature 106th Legislature, 1st Session

Legislation in the 106th Legislature, 1st Session, targeted North Omaha through infrastructure studies, healthcare funding, and economic development tools. LB294 (passed May 2019) allocated $220,000 annually for a minority health satellite office in the second congressional district and authorized feasibility studies for an additional Missouri River bridge and a retaining wall near North Omaha historic markers [1-4]. LB411 overhauled governance by restructuring elections for the Metropolitan Utilities District (MUD) board, which serves the area [5-7].

Economic initiatives centered on the "extremely blighted" designation used to define North Omaha neighborhoods. LB648 (introduced Jan 2019) proposed extending tax-increment financing (TIF) repayment from 15 to 20 years to stimulate investment in these areas [8, 9]. LB683 directed a management work plan for the Omaha Public Schools’ retirement system [10]. Proposals that did not pass included the Density Bonus and Inclusionary Housing Act (LB136), the Nebraska Education Formula (LB656) aimed at school funding, and the Healthy Kids Act (LB653) requiring lead-hazard housing inspections [11-14].

Nebraska Legislature 105th Legislature, 2nd Session

During the 105th Legislature, 2nd Session, legislation targeted North Omaha’s socio-economic landscape through poverty-focused funding and redevelopment reform. LB 874 mandates that 10% of Convention Center Support Fund assistance for Omaha be distributed to high-poverty areas—defined by census tracts with over 30% poverty—to showcase historical sites or reduce gang violence [1-3]. LB 614 (Approved Feb 14, 2018) further shaped the area by removing the $5 million annual cap on municipal economic development appropriations [4, 5]. Furthermore, budget provisions maintained the City of the Metropolitan Class Development Fund for riverfront redevelopment projects north of Interstate 480 [6, 7].

Localized proposals focused on structural hazards: LB 1028 introduced the Abandoned and Dilapidated Housing Act to restore neighborhoods [8, 9], while LB 1060 targeted lead-based hazards in older residential housing [10]. However, LB 1085, which sought to extend Tax-Increment Financing (TIF) repayment periods to 20 years for "extremely blighted" properties, was indefinitely postponed [11-13]. Additionally, LB 944 supported critical regional infrastructure, including funding for community health centers serving the area [14].

Nebraska Legislature 105th Legislature, 1st Session

The 105th Legislature focused on North Omaha through targeted housing reforms and economic initiatives. LB 159 was enacted in May 2017, authorizing Omaha to extend repayment periods for special assessments—such as sewers and sidewalks—up to twenty years for amounts exceeding $5,000 [1-3]. LB 399 modernized the Nebraska Housing Agency Act, specifically requiring that Omaha’s housing board include at least one minority member and professionals with expertise in real estate, law, or finance [4-6].

Economic proposals included LB 616, the Startup Nebraska Program Act, which sought to establish tax-free zones for new businesses in "historically underutilized business zones" [7, 8]. Social initiatives like LR 98 expressed support for the "My Brother’s Keeper" initiative to improve outcomes for young men of color [9], while LR 207 initiated a study on Douglas County’s public health disparities regarding drug usage and STDs [10]. Additionally, LB 496 was introduced to allow workforce housing TIF projects in "extremely blighted areas," defined by high unemployment [11-13]. LR 244 honored the legacy of North Omaha labor leader Edward Danner [14].
