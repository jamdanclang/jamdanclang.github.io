Nebraska Legislature 109th Legislature, 2nd Session

During the 109th Legislature, Second Session, the Nebraska Legislature passed LB1086, which ensures that individuals participating in the SNAP Employment and Training (Next Step) program are not disqualified from receiving community college GAP assistance [1, 2].

Several other significant SNAP-related proposals were introduced but did not pass:

  • LB734: Proposed directing the Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS) to seek federal waivers to opt Nebraska out of work requirements established under federal H.R.1, specifically to restore SNAP exemptions for veterans, homeless individuals, and former foster youth under age 24 [3, 4].
  • LB843: Proposed instructing DHHS to apply for a federal waiver to restore SNAP eligibility for refugees and immigrants who were excluded under federal H.R.1 [5, 6].
  • LB723: Proposed requiring DHHS to utilize SNAP data to automatically assess and verify compliance or exemption status for Medicaid expansion community engagement requirements [7, 8].

Nebraska Legislature 109th Legislature, 1st Session

During the session, several legislative actions modified SNAP benefits.

LB192 (approved June 2, 2025) changed SNAP eligibility and reporting rules [109A-1]. Through amendments AM1131/AM1225, the bill restricted DHHS from seeking federal waivers for SNAP work requirements under 7 U.S.C. 2015(o), but preserved the state’s option to grant exemptions under 7 U.S.C. 2015(o)(6) [2, 3]. It also allowed discretionary mandates for employment and training programs [109A-2].

LB319 opted Nebraska out of the federal lifetime SNAP ban for drug-related felonies, making individuals eligible if they completed their sentences or are under active supervision [109A-4]. However, those with three or more convictions must complete or participate in substance abuse treatment [109A-5].

Additionally, revenue bill LB647 excluded educational savings, postsecondary scholarships, and work-study income from SNAP eligibility assessments [109A-6].

Conversely, LB46, which proposed establishing a Restaurant Meals Program for elderly, disabled, and homeless SNAP recipients to redeem benefits at contracting restaurants, did not pass [7, 8].

Nebraska Legislature 107th Legislature, 2nd Session

LB 121 proposed extensive SNAP reforms, directing the state to maximize federal options [107B-1]. Under its amendments, it increased the gross income eligibility limit to 165% of federal poverty guidelines (funded with federal funds through September 30, 2023) [2, 3]. It sought to eliminate general asset limits while implementing a \$25,000 liquid asset cap [3, 4]. Significantly, LB 121 opted Nebraska out of the federal SNAP ban for individuals with drug felony convictions, granting eligibility if they completed their sentence or are on parole, probation, or post-release supervision [4, 5].

Among proposals that did not advance as far, LB 356 proposed a drug felon ban opt-out that excluded individuals with three or more convictions or distribution charges [107B-6]. LB 857 proposed using SNAP data for Medicaid and CHIP "express lane" eligibility starting January 1, 2023 [107B-7]. Finally, LB 918 adjusted criminal penalties for SNAP fraud, classifying the unauthorized use or transfer of benefits over \$3,000 as a Class IV felony [107B-8].

Nebraska Legislature 107th Legislature, 1st Session

The Legislature enacted LB108 on May 26, 2021, passing it over the Governor's veto [107A-1]. This law increased Nebraska's SNAP gross income eligibility limit from 130% to 165% of the federal poverty level through September 30, 2023 [2-5]. It eliminated most asset limits, capped allowable liquid assets at \$25,000 [4, 6, 7], and modified the state's opt-out of the federal drug felony ban [6, 7]. Under LB108, individuals with one or two felony convictions for controlled substance possession or use are eligible for SNAP benefits if they participate in or complete an accredited substance abuse treatment program [6-9].

In contrast, LB121 did not pass [10, 11]. It proposed a broader removal of the SNAP ban for individuals with drug-related felony convictions who had completed their sentences or were actively serving terms of parole, probation, or post-release supervision [12, 13].

Nebraska Legislature 106th Legislature, 2nd Session

During the 106th Legislative Session, LB 169 was introduced to maximize SNAP participation [106B-1]. Under committee amendment AM710, Nebraska opted out of the federal lifetime SNAP ban for individuals with controlled substance felony convictions [2, 3]. Eligible individuals must have completed their sentence or be serving probation or parole, and those with one or two possession convictions must complete or participate in a substance abuse treatment program [3, 4]. However, those with three or more possession convictions or any distribution convictions remained ineligible [4, 5].

Other proposals did not pass. LB 255 proposed increasing the SNAP gross income eligibility limit from 130% to 140% of the federal poverty guidelines [6-8]. LB 402 similarly sought to eliminate certain SNAP eligibility restrictions [106B-9]. Additionally, the Legislature introduced interim study LR 364 to examine the potential impact of plastic shopping bag fees on low-income individuals and SNAP beneficiaries [106B-10].

Nebraska Legislature 106th Legislature, 1st Session

During this session, several proposed bills sought to expand eligibility for the federal Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP), though none are recorded as having passed.

LB169 (introduced Jan 11, 2019, by Hunt) proposed to modify the federal drug-felony ban on food assistance [1, 2]. Under committee amendment AM710, individuals with one or two drug possession or use felony convictions could qualify if they completed or were serving their sentence and participated in drug treatment, while those with three or more convictions or any distribution convictions remained ineligible [3, 4]. LB402 (introduced Jan 17, 2019, by Hilkemann) similarly proposed a complete opt-out of the federal SNAP ban for past drug convictions [5-7].

To address the "cliff effect," LB255 (introduced Jan 14, 2019, by McCollister) proposed raising the SNAP gross income eligibility limit from 130% to 140% of the Federal Poverty Level [8-10].

Additionally, Senator Chambers repeatedly introduced amendments (such as AM1139 to LB212, AM1142 to LB243, AM1143 to LB472, and AM1146 to LB590) to replace unrelated bill texts with LB169's drug-felony SNAP provisions [11-14].

Nebraska Legislature 105th Legislature, 2nd Session

During the 105th Legislature, several measures proposed modifications to Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP) benefits and eligibility [1-4]:

  • Income Eligibility: LB358 (2017) sought to increase the gross income limit to 158% of the Federal Poverty Level (FPL) for FY2017–18, though committee amendment AM493 struck a further proposed increase to 185% [3, 5, 6]. LB770 (2018) subsequently proposed raising this limit to 160% of the FPL to counter the work-disincentive "cliff effect" [4, 7].
  • Asset Limits: LB224 (2017) aimed to streamline public assistance by eliminating standard SNAP asset limits and establishing a \$25,000 liquid asset cap [1, 8].
  • Drug Felony Restrictions: LB311 (2017) proposed completely opting out of the federal ban on SNAP eligibility for individuals with past drug felonies [2, 9]. Conversely, LB128 (2017) proposed permitting eligibility for these individuals only under strict conditions, such as completing substance abuse treatment or passing regular, random drug tests [10, 11].

None of these bills are recorded as having passed in the session's final acts.

Nebraska Legislature 105th Legislature, 1st Session

During this legislative session, several changes to Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP) benefits were proposed, though none of the major eligibility reforms passed into law:

  • Drug Felonies & Eligibility: LB128 proposed requiring substance abuse treatment or random drug testing for individuals with past drug felonies to qualify for SNAP [1, 2]. Conversely, LB311 proposed completely opting Nebraska out of the federal SNAP ban for drug felons [3, 4].
  • Asset & Income Limits: LB224 proposed aligning SNAP asset limits with the Child Care Subsidy program [5, 6]. LB358 sought to address the SNAP "cliff effect" by raising the gross income eligibility limit to 158% of the federal poverty level (FPL) in FY2017–18 and 185% FPL in FY2018–19 [7, 8]. It was advanced but amended (AM493) to cap the limit at 158% [9, 10].
  • Incentives & Deductions: LB260 proposed a state food insecurity nutrition incentive grant program [105A-11]. Finally, unemployment bill LB172 proposed deducting SNAP overissuances from unemployment benefits [12, 13].
